COERCIVE CONTROL

Coercive Control is not a distinct form of domestic and family violence; it is an underpinning dynamic of it.

It describes a pattern of abusive behaviours used to control, manipulate and dominate.

These tactics instill fear in people who are abused, wear down their sense of identity and independence, and entrap them by disabling them from accessing safety and support.

Tactics of Coercive Control

Select on each tactic to learn more.

Physical Violence

This can include hitting, punching, pushing, restraining, or any other form of physical harm. It also includes threatening to cause harm.

Making Threats

Using intimidation, threats of violence, or threats to harm oneself, children, or pets to instill fear and ensure compliance.

Social Isolation

Preventing contact with friends, family, or support networks. This can involve monitoring calls, checking messages, or making it difficult to leave the house.

Controlling Choices

Dictating everyday decisions, such as what to wear, where to go, who to see, or what to study/work. This removes a person's autonomy.

Monitoring & Stalking

Using technology (like trackers or apps) or in-person surveillance to track movements, conversations, and online activity without consent.

Financial Control

Restricting access to money, controlling bank accounts, preventing work or education, or forcing someone to account for every cent spent.
